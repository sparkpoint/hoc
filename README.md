# hoc
A developing framework for peaceful communities, esoterically, economically, and sociologically inspired.
Abstract: Trust is commonly treated as a moral sentiment or a social contract between equals. This
treatise argues it is neither. Trust is domain-specific knowledge: accumulated evidence that a
particular person has acted truthfully in a particular kind of relationship, under pressure, when
deception would have been easier.
Because every person belongs to many groups at once — family, craft, friendship, creed — trust
cannot be assigned to a person whole. It must be assigned to a person within a clade. And
because relationships differ in depth, trust must also be graded along an axis of obligation: Self,
Nuclear Family, Friend/Clanmember, Ally, Enemy, Stranger.
This gives us a two-axis model — the Clade Matrix — where every relationship sits at an
intersection of type and depth. Violation of trust is then not merely a social failure but a psychic
event: either a learning pang necessary for individuation, or the work of a parasitic complex that
feeds on integration without doing the work of achieving it.
